An operations team has limited attention. Frequent delays, busy airports and many delay minutes can point to different places to investigate. This study asks which evidence should guide that choice.

02 / Test a forecast

Can a forecast help choose flights to review?

A separate historical experiment uses flights departing New York in 2013. A baseline is a simple prediction to beat. A random forest combines decision trees using schedule information.

The simple baseline had less average error.

Average absolute error measures the distance from prediction to outcome, in minutes. Lower is better. The median baseline won validation, so model selection was frozen before testing.

The forest's mean-oriented training objective differs from the absolute-error criterion. A more complex model did not improve this measure.

Schedule features, leakage and model settings

Features: reporting carrier, departure and arrival airport, scheduled local hour, date-derived weekday and route distance. Actual departure/arrival times, actual delays, tail number, air time, delay causes and observed weather are excluded.

One forest: 40 trees, depth 12, minimum leaf 100, seed 42, squared-error training. Training-only category encoding handles unseen categories. No random split, test tuning, refit or hyperparameter search. Schedule feature availability is assumed: the archive contains no authenticated day-ahead snapshots.

04 / Check the uncertainty

How trustworthy are the proposed ranges?

Coverage means how often a measured delay falls inside the proposed range. The selected median baseline's interval aimed at 90% coverage. It missed that target on test, with weaker coverage in December.

Check uncertainty over time.

These ranges belong to the selected median baseline. A nominal coverage target is not a flight-level probability of reliability. This experiment does not identify the cause of December's change.

06 / Follow the evidence

Methods, sources and reproducibility

Implemented: monthly audit, evidence export and static research page. Experimental: historical forecasts and split-conformal ranges. Planned: current data, alert-focused model selection, rolling tests and prospective customer evaluation.

Data sources, rights and provenance

BTS delay causes supplies monthly US arrival aggregates. Delayed means at least 15 minutes after scheduled arrival. Cause counts are prorated. Raw BTS redistribution rights were not established; only derived findings are published. Local acquisition history is partly verified.

nycflights13 pinned data supplies 2013 NYC outbound flights, originally from BTS. Upstream metadata specifies CC0. Source URL, retrieval, conversion and hashes are in the bundle. Raw files, CVs, credentials and private artifacts are excluded.

Metric definitions and validation

Rate sums known delayed counts and divides by flights on those same records. It is not an average of percentages. Delay minutes sum available burden; missing is not zero. Duplicate keys, invalid counts, negative/nonfinite values and invalid periods fail before new reports are written. Cause inconsistencies are flagged, not corrected.

Test MAE, queue accounting and month-level interval coverage are independently reported. Exports reconcile full population totals, all three rankings and capacity-20 results to frozen reports. Synthetic tests demonstrate behavior; CI does not re-acquire the private local BTS download.

Future study protocol and role relevance

Freeze this v1 experiment. Acquire bounded recent data, add a historical severe-rate baseline, predeclare an alert-focused validation criterion, test multiple chronological windows and improve unknown-outcome handling. Join weather forecasts and rotation evidence as-of a real decision timestamp.

This independent project can support an Analytics Engineer discovery and feasibility discussion: define the decision, inspect data gaps, evaluate trade-offs and communicate the next evidence request. It is not an affiliation with NABLA Mobility or a verified internal product need.
