Which delays deserve attention?

A study of where delays accumulate, how forecasts compare, and what a limited review team can find.

Read the study ↓
The study in brief

The baseline had lower prediction error. The forest found more severe delays within a fixed review budget. Uncertainty ranges missed their coverage target. Together, these results show why forecast accuracy alone cannot establish usefulness.

01 / Problem and research questions

High traffic, frequent delays and accumulated delay time can identify different priorities. This study compares them, then tests forecasts for selecting flights to review.

02 / Literature and study focus

What prior work informs this study?

Interpret reported causes carefully.

BTS definitions distinguish weather, airspace-system and late-aircraft categories. These are reporting groups, not complete causal explanations.

Check the range, not just the prediction.

Angelopoulos and Bates inform the interval method. This study checks how often later outcomes fall within the proposed ranges.

Network effects remain a next step.

Sadeek, Hanaoka and Sugishita (2026) study network properties and conformal prediction. Their work motivates future extensions; those methods are not reproduced here.

Study focus: evaluate error, review usefulness and uncertainty separately. A field-wide research gap has not been established.

Researcher detail: comparison matrix and reading status

This is a selective review, not a systematic literature review. The aviation paper was reviewed through citation and publisher snippets; full methods and proprietary data were not reproduced. A fuller review should compare targets, datasets, evaluation and access status.

04 / Methods and rationale

How was the study tested?

Audit and compare.

SQL checks missing counts and cause totals. Rankings compare delay frequency, accumulated minutes and flight volume.

Test against a baseline.

The median baseline predicts one fixed value. The forest uses schedule information. Average absolute error measures how far each prediction is from the outcome, in minutes.

Test on later flights.

Earlier data train and select the model. Later outcomes remain untouched until testing.

Jan–JunTrain
Jul–AugChoose
Sep–OctCalibrate
Nov–DecTest

Review budgets: 10, 20, 40 or 80 flights per day. Severe delay: departure at least 60 minutes late.

Technical detail: features, forest settings, tie rules and conformal formula

No random split or test tuning. The forest uses 40 trees, depth 12, minimum leaf size 100 and seed 42. Its squared-error training differs from MAE selection. Split-conformal intervals use an order statistic of absolute calibration residuals. Temporal dependence can undermine exchangeability. Constant baseline scores use deterministic schedule-key ties, giving an arbitrary control queue.

05 / Results

The baseline had lower error.

The baseline won model selection and had lower error on the later test flights.

In the separate arrival study, Delta–Atlanta led eligible delay totals: 450,182 minutes. The total reflects traffic as well as disruption. Explore the full ranking ↗

The forest found more severe delays.

At 20 reviews/day, the forest found 239 severe delays; the baseline found 120. Each reviewed 1,220 flights. Most severe delays were missed.

The ranges missed their target.

Target coverage: 90%. Observed: 85.45%, falling to 80.55% in December. The baseline range was 56 minutes wide.

Model selection stays fixed. A future review-focused study needs criteria agreed before testing.

06 / Discussion and contribution

What can someone use this for?

The project turns public delay records into an investigation shortlist and tests whether forecasts can help a team decide which flights to review.

01 / LOCATE

Choose where to investigate.

Compare airport–airline combinations by delay time, frequency and traffic. The ranking points to questions worth checking, not their causes.

02 / TEST

Check whether a model adds value.

The simple baseline had lower average error. The forest found more severe delays at the same review budget. Model choice depends on the decision.

03 / INSPECT

Measure workload and missed delays.

The demo shows reviews used, severe delays found and delays missed. Coverage checks also show when prediction ranges fall short.

This is a reproducible evaluation tool, not a proven delay-reduction system. Current airline relevance, passenger impact and intervention benefits remain untested.
